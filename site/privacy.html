Privacy

Last updated November 13, 2026

Loner-Strike is made by Nanoshrine Interactive LLC, a California limited liability company ("we"). This explains what this website, the Loner-Strike app and our online services do with information about you. Short version: we don't use cookies, we don't track you, and we don't sell anything about you.

This website

  • Visitor statistics. We count visits with Plausible Analytics, which we run on our own server. It uses no cookies and stores no personal data: no IP addresses, no identifiers that follow you around. We see totals like how many people visited, which pages, which country, and which buttons were clicked. That's why there's no cookie banner.
  • Hosting. The site is served by Cloudflare Pages. Like any web host, Cloudflare handles your browser's requests, which include your IP address, to deliver the page and protect it from attacks. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.
  • Checkout script. The "Name your price" button loads Polar's checkout script from jsDelivr, a public code CDN, which sees your IP address when it sends the file. See jsDelivr's privacy policy.
  • Visitor counter and server status. Once per visit the page adds one to a visitor count, and while it's open it asks our game server what's playing. Both come from status.lonerstrike.com, which keeps only the count itself: no cookies, and no IP addresses stored.
  • Discord. After the page loads, it asks discord.com for the Discord invite and how many people are online, so Discord sees that request under its own privacy policy.
  • Radio. The optional radio player streams from the station you pick (Bassdrive, Kniteforce, TechnoBase.FM, HardBase.FM, SomaFM, SceneSat, Rainwave, 0nlineradio or Radio Bob) when you press play, and while it plays asks the station what's on every 20 seconds, so those stations see your IP address like any listener.
  • Fonts and images are served from this site itself, not from Google or anyone else.
  • Do Not Track. We don't track you across sites, so a browser's "Do Not Track" signal changes nothing here.

Paying

Paying is optional. If you do, the payment is handled by Polar, which acts as the merchant of record: Polar processes your payment, sends your receipt and handles sales tax. Polar shares your name, email address, country and what you paid with us, so we can help if something goes wrong. We never see your card details. See Polar's privacy policy.

The Loner-Strike app

The app runs on your computer and sends nothing about you to us. There's no account and no telemetry. It only goes online to:

  • check GitHub for updates, and download the mods you install from their original sources;
  • browse and download maps and other content from GameBanana, when you use those pages;
  • connect to services you choose to use for playing with friends, such as playit.gg, Tailscale, your router (UPnP) or Valve's server downloader;
  • connect over SSH to Linux servers you rent, when you add one;
  • download the speech models for voice commands, when you install them, and the chat models for Smarter chat, if you turn it on (both from Hugging Face; the models then run on your computer);
  • download ready-made bot navigation for maps, and other optional files, from our download server (dl.lonerstrike.com);
  • ask our server list (list.lonerstrike.com) for servers when you open Find servers, and tell it about a server you choose to list there (its address; the list then asks the server itself for its name, map and player count);
  • download the SDHLT map compiler from GitHub, the first time you compile a map;
  • use Community, described below.

Voice commands are optional and off until you turn them on for a server. Players' team voice is turned into text on the machine running that server, to give the bots their orders. No audio is kept or sent anywhere; the lines it heard are written to that server's own log.

Our own servers (dl.lonerstrike.com, list.lonerstrike.com and status.lonerstrike.com) sit behind Cloudflare, so Cloudflare also handles the app's downloads and list requests and sees your IP address, as it does for this website. Each of those services has its own privacy policy. Your settings, admin lists, server passwords, SSH keys and the list of players who joined your games stay on your computer, and on the servers you run. If you run a server, what it logs is yours to look after (see the terms).

The optional connector for AI agents only listens on your own computer. Loner-Strike sends nothing to any AI service; the agent you connect is a program you run, under its own terms.

Our game server

We run a public Counter-Strike server, play.lonerstrike.com, hosted by Hetzner. Like any game server, it sees and logs players' IP addresses, Steam IDs, names and chat, and, if voice commands are on, the text it heard from team voice (never the audio). We use those logs to deal with cheating, abuse and problems, and don't share them.

Community

Community lists maps and modes people made with Loner-Strike. The files are hosted by GameBanana, under its own privacy policy, and the app never signs in there for you.

  • Browsing. The app downloads the Community list from dl.lonerstrike.com. No account, and nothing about you is sent.
  • Publishing. Adding a creation sends us its GameBanana page address and the app's version. The listing is built from what's public on GameBanana: the title, the GameBanana name and member number of who made it, its pictures and its file, plus the credits in the package.
  • Reports. A report sends the page, the reason and your note. We keep them for 90 days. To spot repeated reports from one person, the server keeps a scrambled tag of your IP address made with a key that only lives in memory and changes when it restarts; the address itself is never stored, and moderators never see it.
  • Limits and logs. Request limits are counted per address in memory only. Logs say what happened to which page, not who asked.

More detail is in the docs.

What our community server keeps

The Community and server list service runs on our own server, hosted by Hetzner, and keeps a small database. Here's everything in it that's about a person:

  • Creators. For each listing, the GameBanana member number and name of who registered it. We use them to show who made it and to tell whose new version is whose.
  • Credits. The names, roles and GameBanana links of the people credited for the assets a creation uses, and any permission note the creator recorded. These come from the package and from public GameBanana pages.
  • Permanent ids. Which GameBanana member holds each creation's permanent id, when they got it, and any disputes over it and how they were settled.
  • Moderation history. What was listed, hidden, pulled, approved or turned down and why, which creators are blocked and why, and which moderator did it (their Discord id and name). We keep each entry for 2 years, to handle disputes and repeat problems, then delete it. A block stays on record for as long as it's in place, and its history is deleted 2 years after it's lifted. Moderators' notification cards and permanent-id disputes are also kept for 2 years.
  • Creations that leave the list. A creation that's pulled, turned down or hidden is deleted 2 years later, together with its creator details, credits and permanent-id records. Ownership records for a creation that's gone are deleted with it.
  • Reports, as described above, for 90 days.
  • Role applications. When you apply for a role on our Discord, we keep your Discord user id and name, the role, what you wrote (why, and any links), the date you joined our Discord, and the decision: who made it, when, and any reason. We delete an application 180 days after it's decided, or 180 days after it was sent if it never was.
  • Discord. Moderation cards, reports and applications also appear in our private Discord moderation channels, which we clean up from time to time.
  • Backups. The database is backed up once a day on the same server, and each backup is kept for 14 days. So something we delete can stay in a backup for up to 14 more days.

The public Community list (the index the app downloads) is published every few minutes by a small program on Cloudflare (a Worker), which copies it from our server to the download server. It carries only what the list shows anyway: creations, the GameBanana names and member numbers of their makers, and credits. Reports, applications, moderation records and anything about people who browse never go through it.

Our Discord bot

Our moderators work through buttons and forms in our Discord. When someone presses one of the bot's buttons or sends one of its forms (including "Apply for a role"), Discord sends our server that person's Discord user id, name and roles in our server, the date they joined it, which button they pressed, which message it was on, and what they typed in the form. We check that the request really came from Discord, and that the person may use that button. We keep the id of each press for one day so it can't be done twice, and record who acted on each card as part of the moderation history above. When an application is turned down, the bot may send the applicant a direct message saying so.

California privacy rights

If you live in California, the California Consumer Privacy Act (as amended by the CPRA) may give you these rights, and we honor them for everyone regardless.

  • What we collect, in the law's categories: identifiers (GameBanana and Discord ids and names; Polar's name, email address and country for people who pay); commercial information (what you paid, from Polar); and the things you write to us (report notes, role applications, credits). The sources, purposes and how long we keep them are described above. We don't collect sensitive personal information, and we don't use it to infer anything about you.
  • We don't sell or share your personal information, as the law defines those words, and haven't in the past 12 months. We don't use it for targeted advertising.
  • You can ask to know what we collected about you, and to correct or delete it. You can use someone you authorize to ask for you. We won't treat you differently for asking.
  • How to ask: email legal@nanoshrineinteractive.com, message a moderator privately on our Discord, or reply to your Polar receipt email. We'll answer within 45 days.

Children

Our services aren't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. If we learn we have, we delete it. If you think a child under 13 has sent us something, tell us.
